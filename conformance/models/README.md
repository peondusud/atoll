# The TLA+ model

Atoll's worst bugs are interleavings: Atoll killed between two syncs of a move while a USB disk vanishes and a pass propagates a deletion. Tests check the orders someone thought of; TLC, the model checker of TLA+, explores every reachable state of a small pool and gives the shortest trace to each violation (the maintainer, 2026-11-19, `specs.md` §27). The model is language-neutral, as the vectors are: the Rust version inherits it.

Six modules hold it: `Replication.tla`, the daemon's passes, and since 2026-11-22 Atoll's own view, which writes beside them, the SSD cache's transfer and the link guard, together since 2026-11-23; `Rename.tla`, since that day, renames and folders (below, Renames and folders); `Copies.tla`, copies at once, and since 2026-11-23 of one file to several disks with its refreshes (below, Copies at once); and since 2026-11-23 `Folders.tla`, folders renamed across disks with their jobs, chains, cycles and exchanges (below, Folders renamed across disks), `ViewMove.tla`, the move of a file the view writes that ran out of space (below, The view's move of a file out of space), and `ViewNames.tla`, the view's renames, its replacements onto a name it presents and its folders renamed or removed on every disk at once (below, Atoll's own view, its renames and folders). The view, the cache and the guard are extensions of the first rather than modules of their own, as what each does is only worth checking against the passes that take it in: their variables and steps stay as they start in an instance whose `ViewActs` is empty, whose `CacheDisks` is empty and whose `Guard` is `"none"`, so that every instance written before them explores the very states it did.

## Running it

```bash
make model-check                         # every instance, each against what it expects
test/tla/run.sh Copy.cfg Live.cfg        # some of them
make model-flaws                         # the black-box scenarios of the flaws still open, and their tests: flaws 18 to 22's
```

`test/tla/run.sh` needs only Docker: it runs TLC in `eclipse-temurin:21.0.13_9-jre-noble`, with 2 workers, a 2 GiB heap and 2 CPUs, so that it leaves the machine to whatever else runs there. It downloads `tla2tools.jar` 1.7.4 once into `bin/tla/`, git-ignored, and checks its SHA-256. Each instance's log, with the full trace of a violation, goes to `bin/tla/<instance>.log`. With `--inside`, it runs TLC where it is, in that image, as the CI's `tla` job and `test/ci/run.sh` do.

The first line of each `.cfg` says what TLC must find there: `\* expect: pass`, or `\* expect: <invariant>` for a flaw of `specs.md` that the model found and that waits for the maintainer's remedy. An instance that finds anything else fails `make model-check`, a flaw fixed included: its expectation then becomes `pass`.

## The model

**The world.** Disks hold objects, each standing for an inode with its creation time (the identity of §15.1), with a content (a version number), a change count (what size, modification and change time say), and a count of what moved the change time alone: a rename, or a write by a program that keeps the size and the dates. Each disk has its names twice: as the kernel shows them, and as they would survive a power cut, so that what a sync makes durable is explicit. The names are a file's logical path, a part `<op-id>.part` in `<workdir>/tmp/`, and an entry `<date>/<op-id>/…` in `<workdir>/trash/`. A disk is up, or gone: answering `EIO`, or an empty mount point; mounted again it is a new mount (a new mount id) and has lost what it had not made durable, as XFS brings a rename back after a remount (§17.1). A user writes into a copy (a new version, same inode), or rewrites it keeping its size and dates (a new version, same inode, only the change time moved), replaces it (an atomic save: a new inode), deletes it, or makes a file where none was, on any disk at any moment, durably, with what came before on that disk. Atoll is killed (kill -9: the catalog keeps what it committed, the process loses the rest), or the power is cut (every disk keeps only what was durable).

**Atoll.** One process that runs passes, step by step, as the conformance build does, one copy at a time (§29.4): the mount check of every disk and the session's confirmation, which binds the session's handles to that mount (§10); the reprise of each disk's planned operations, in two steps, what it does on the disk then its commit (§17.2, §14.2 Reprise); a part in `tmp/` no job owns, dropped (§14.2); the walk of each disk, then its confirmation, and the verdicts there, edited, replaced, gone, own and unknown (§15.10, §15.12); the decisions Deletion, Kept and Gone (§15.12), with peers, which wait while a job of the file is running (§14.1); the modifications, `modified`, `metadata` and `conflict`, with their waits (§15.4); a stale copy's trash job queued again (§15.4); the trash jobs and the operation `trash` step by step, with `link` and `unlink` on a disk without `RENAME_NOREPLACE`, and the look after the move, which resolves at once, as a reprise would, a move whose name in the trash no longer holds what the copy's row says (§15.12, §17.1, step 4); the plan, which keeps or cancels pending copies, counts the copies, finds a source, and places a copy where no disk on the same physical disk holds one (§13.4, §14.1); the copies, the operation `create` step by step: journaled, the part made and its inode recorded, copied, the source looked at again, synced, verified, renamed without replacing anything and looked at, the change time that look found recorded in the job (`placed`), the file then its folder synced, the folder of `tmp/` synced, the commit (§14, §14.2). A disk that fails at any step leaves its operation planned for the reprise. A decision may always wait, which stands for the debounce, `settle` and the guards.

**Atoll's own view** (§21.2, `internal/view/writer`): `atoll mount`, a process of its own beside the daemon, one operation at a time, as the writer's lock serializes them, each journaled in `view_ops` before it runs, with its operation of `ops` in the same transaction. A user, through it, makes a file where it presents nothing, on a disk it can write to, which a program then writes into and closes; opens a file it presents to write, its write journaled once on the copy it presents; and deletes the copy it presents, sent to the trash at once as §17.1 sends one: the look right before the move, taken apart from the move so that a user may act between them directly on the disk, the move or `link`, its sync, the look at the source and `unlink`, the look after the move, the syncs and the commit. A new file with `Parallel` gets a part on its replica's target, the solver's (one part, at `Level` 2): journaled (an operation `create` of `ops` and a row `part`), made and its inode recorded, given each write the program makes or failing as one that falls behind, sealed against the original's size once the program closes the file, then handed over, `done`, its operation left planned; a part that fails is dropped, or left planned on a disk that cannot be relied on. The view has its own handles, opened by its own mount check at its start and again later, which a disk lost and mounted again makes answer `EIO`. Stopped short (kill -9, `MaxStop`), or by a power cut, it loses its parts and its open file, and at its next start resolves what it left planned (`VRepriseOp`). The daemon's reprise leaves the operations a row of `view_ops` names alone; its walks wait at the paths of the view's operations still planned there and take a copy the view sent to the trash for a user's deletion, not Atoll's own; before any job runs, a pass gives each part handed over to the replicate job of its file on that disk queued with no part yet, which resumes from it, its operation undone and its row taken in; once the jobs ran, it drops a part no job took; and it ends by taking in each operation of the view, in the order of their ids, once the catalog alone presents at its path what the view presents with them. What a path presents is the latest operation of the view not taken in there, a file made or written presenting its disk and a deletion nothing, or else the catalog's original, the copy `ok` with its clock not running on a disk online with the oldest creation time, which the order the model makes its objects in stands for.

**The SSD cache's transfer** (§19, `internal/jobs/transfer.go`, `trash.Session.Transfer`, `watch.CacheTransfers`): with `CacheDisks`, a file with a copy on a cache disk is resident there or leaves it, which a plan may find either way while decisions may wait, and leaves it otherwise; its copies there count toward no level and stay sources, a cache disk is no candidate, and the view's new files land on it, written there alone. After the copies, each file that leaves the cache is planned again alone, from the catalog: once its copies on data disks that count reach its level, each `ok`, verified, at its path and on a disk online, with no job and no pending copy, its copy on the cache, `ok`, at its path and on a disk online, goes, unless an operation of the view no pass has taken in touches its path. A user copy there hands its part to a copy on a data disk first (`catalog.HandOver`, a transaction of its own), then the move is journaled, `surplus`, the copy compared with its row right before the move (one step with the move), moved, synced, committed, and the entry purged at once: an operation `unlink` journaled, the file in the trash looked at by its inode and unlinked, the folder synced, the commit. Each step may be the last before a kill, a power cut or a disk lost; the reprise of the purge finishes when nothing is in the trash's name and undoes it otherwise. With the link guard (since 2026-11-23), the purge first drops the link of the guard that leads to its entry, as `trash.Session.dropLinksOf` does: an operation `unlink` of the guard journaled, the link removed when the entry is another name of its data, its folder synced, committed, then the purge, whatever that removal ended in; and the trash's keys a link is decided against are those of the trash's rows, which a purge done removes, as `catalog.TrashKeys` reads them. The time of the residency, `replication.window`, `max_fill`, `cache.mirror` and `cache.replicate_early` are left out.

**The link guard** (§17, `trash.Session.Guard`, `solver.PlanLinks`, `internal/ops/linkguard.go`): with `Guard` `"on"`, or `"off"`, on at the start and turned off once by the user at any moment, each disk has a link at `<workdir>/links/<file id>`, another name of the same object. Once everything else of a pass ran, on each disk it walked and can write to, each file's link is decided against the catalog, as `PlanLinks` decides it: kept when it leads to its file's copy, or with the guard off removed, but for a copy the last look did not find there; removed when its data is another copy found there or an entry of the trash, the removal refused when the link is its data's last name; sent to the trash from the link otherwise, `overwritten` when the file still has a copy on that disk, `deleted` otherwise, at the file's path, but only removed when the library's name still holds that data; then the copy found there without its link gets one. Each operation (`link`, `unlink` with a link, `trash` from a link) journaled, made, the links folder synced, committed, the copy's row taking the change time the link gave it when it recorded the one just before (§17.1, step 6); the reprise of each as §17.2 says, a move from a link by the table with the link as its source and nothing edited in place. A walk counts no link as one of Atoll's own places, so that a copy whose data only its link holds is gone by the user (invariant 11), and does not wait at the guard's planned operations (`jobs.plannedOn`).

**The catalog.** Files (their hash as the version it describes, conflict), copies (state `ok`, `stale` or `pending`, inode, change count, `verified_ns`, `created_by`, `gone_kind`, `first_seen_ns` as a clock), the jobs `replicate` and `trash`, the journal `ops` (`planned`, `done`, `undone`; of the kinds `create`, `trash`, from a copy or from a link of the guard, `unlink`, a purge or a link's removal, and `link`), each disk's state from the last mount check, and the view's journal `view_ops` (kind, disk, operation of `ops`, file key, the look a deletion compares its copy with, state, `taken_ns`).

**The invariants**, with the numbers of `CLAUDE.md`'s non-negotiable invariants:

| Invariant | Checks |
|---|---|
| `NoUserDataLost` | every version a user wrote, or the library held at the start, is still durably on some disk, in the library, a trash or a link of the guard, missing disks included, unless a user's own action took its last copy there; a version a user's action leaves only in an entry of the SSD cache's transfer, which it purges at once, or that the kernel no longer shows at another name, is the user's to lose |
| `Inv1_LastVerifiedCopyKept` | 1: the version the catalog vouches for is still on some disk; Atoll never makes a name hold something else |
| `Inv2_LastUserCopyKept` | 2: the one removal no command asks for, the SSD cache's transfer, moves out of the library only an Atoll copy, its user part handed over first, and the file keeps a user copy |
| `Inv4_UnknownNeverAbsent` | 4: nothing a walk of a disk gone (`EIO`, or an empty mount point, mounted again or not) found is recorded |
| `Inv7_PipelineOrder` | 6 and 7: no copy of Atoll's at a name of the library without its content durable and verified, and no commit of a copy or of a move to the trash before the names are durable, the view's deletions and their reprise included, a move to the trash done only once the copy's name in the library is gone |
| `Inv10_DeletionOnlyUnchangedToTrash` | 10: a deletion sends to the trash only a copy unchanged since it was in sync, and never the latest version a user wrote and did not delete (a replacement is no deletion), nor leaves it there, a deletion through the view included |
| `Inv11_OwnOpsNotUsers` | 11: no copy that Atoll's own trash or `tmp/` holds is decided gone, and no copy Atoll made sits in the library unknown to the catalog, where the next walk would take it for a user's new file |
| `Inv12_JournalFirst` | 12: every part in a `tmp/` and every entry in a trash, visible or durable, is named by a committed operation of that disk |
| `Inv13_OnePhysicalDisk` | 13: no plan queues a copy where a disk on the same physical disk holds a copy of the file |
| `ReplicaInSync` | a copy Atoll made, that the catalog calls `ok` and that nobody touched since its row was written, holds the version the catalog says is current (G2), but for one a reprise recorded unverified as its change time may be a write's (§14.2, the limit of flaw 1) |
| `DoubtUnverified` | a copy a reprise recorded so is never vouched for: it stays unverified until a read takes it again |
| `ViewPresentsLatest` | 14: Atoll's own view presents the in-sync copy with the oldest creation time, never a stale one: a file a user last wrote through the view, and did not delete since, reads through it as what was written, while every disk is there and online in the catalog and the file is in no conflict (a change made directly on a disk leaves no claim, as the passes bring it to the view) |
| `EventuallySettled` | liveness, with fairness: a file below its level reaches it unless in conflict, and no operation stays planned; with the SSD cache, every file leaves it, but one whose copy on a data disk waits unverified for a read (§14.2), which the model leaves out |
| `EventuallyViewTaken` | liveness, with fairness: every operation of the view that happened is taken in by a pass at last |
| `EventuallyUnlinked` | liveness, with fairness: once the link guard is turned off, every link goes at last |

## Flaws found in specs.md and in the code

### Flaw 1: a write into a new replica before its row is written is recorded as verified

Fixed on 2026-11-19, with the remedy the maintainer approved (below): `specs.md` §14.2, `conformance/vectors/create_row.json`, `FlawReplicaEdited.cfg` expecting `pass`, and `../scenarios/replica-edited-before-commit.json`.

`specs.md` §14.2 commits a copy "with what a look at its new name finds", and its reprise, for a target `same` and a part `absent`, "finishes as above, with the hashes the job recorded". The look comes after the rename has put the copy in the library: whatever a user writes into it between the rename and the look is recorded as the copy's own state, `verified_ns` set against the hash of the version it was made from.

1. A file of version 1 on disk 1; the plan queues its replica on disk 2.
2. The operation `create` runs up to `source-synced`: the copy is at its name on disk 2, every sync done.
3. A user writes version 2 into it, in place (directly on disk 2, or through mergerfs, whose first branch may be disk 2).
4. The commit records the copy `ok`, verified, with the size and times of version 2, while the file's hash is version 1's (`ReplicaInSync` broken).
5. Every later look finds the copy as its row says: the edit is never propagated. A scrub then reads a mismatch with unchanged size and times, `corrupt`, and repairs the user's edit away from disk 1, the chunks it held in the trash (against G4); or a later change on disk 1 makes it stale and sends it to the trash.

A crash at `source-synced` makes the window as long as Atoll is down: the reprise records what is there when it runs. In the code: `internal/ops/create.go` (`PlacePart`, whose `movedFrom` looks after the syncs) and `internal/ops/recover.go` (`recoverCreate`, "what is there now is what gets recorded"), then `catalog.FinishCreate`. Reproduced by `FlawReplicaEdited.cfg` and `replica-edited-before-commit.json`, which failed until the remedy.

The remedy (`create-look`, approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-19): the row takes what the part was when it was verified, before the rename (inode, size, modification time), so that a write since reads as `edited` at the next look; the reprise likewise records the part's size and modification time from the job, never what it finds. As it landed: the job records with its whole-file hash what the part was then, its size, times, mode, owner and links (`part_look`), and the row takes it all, but for the change time, which the rename moved: that one comes from a look right after the rename, or in a reprise from its look, and only when the look found nothing else moved; otherwise the part's, which the next look finds moved, later than the row says, a modification and never a `conflict` (§15.4). In the model, whose gen stands for size, times and change time at once, and whose rename and the look after it are one step, the row takes gen 0. What it left: a write that keeps the size, the times, the mode and the owner moves only the change time, which a reprise could not tell from the rename's, and took; the model, where every edit moved the size, could not see it either.

**Its limit, closed** (the maintainer, 2026-11-20, "Les deux"; `specs.md` §14.2, `conformance/vectors/create_reprise.json`, `FlawReplicaRewritten.cfg`, and `../scenarios/replica-edited-keeping-dates-after-record.json` and `-before-record.json`). The model now counts apart what moves the change time alone (`cg`): the rename, the name in `tmp/` a reprise takes away, and a user's `rewrite`, a write that keeps the size and the dates. (b) Right after the rename and its look, the job records the change time that look found, in a commit of its own (`CopyRecord`, `placed`), and a reprise that finds another reads a write since: the row is the part's, and the next walk finds the copy `edited`. (a) A reprise that finds no record, the crash or the disk lost having come between the rename and that commit (`moved`), and a change time other than the part's when it was verified, records the copy unverified: the row's `doubt`, no column, which `ReplicaInSync` looks past and `DoubtUnverified` keeps unverified; its write is then for a read, which the model leaves out, to find a `conflict`. Each was taken out once to see TLC fail: without (b), a rename, its record, a rewrite, a crash and the reprise record the write as the copy (`ReplicaInSync`); without (a), the same with the disk lost before the record. The normal run's window stays: the look comes in the step of the rename, which here is the width of a `stat`, and on a disk without `RENAME_NOREPLACE` the look comes after the `unlink`, so that a rewrite between the link and that look is taken (What it leaves out).

### Flaw 2: a job running is dropped while its operation is still planned

Fixed on 2026-11-19, with the remedy the maintainer approved (below): `specs.md` §9, §13.4, §14.1 and §15.12, `conformance/vectors/plan.json` and `propagation.json` (`running`), `FlawJobDroppedGone.cfg` and `FlawJobDroppedPlan.cfg` expecting `pass`, and `../scenarios/replica-of-deleted-file.json` and `replica-of-old-version.json`.

`specs.md` §14.1 says that a job whose operation the reprise could not conclude "stays running, and does not run: what is at its target may be its own copy, and nothing is decided of it until that operation is". But two decisions drop such a job: the plan cancels a pending copy that no longer counts, "its row goes, with its job" (§13.4, §14.1), as one on a disk `missing` does; and the decision Gone deletes the file, with its jobs (§15.12, §9). Back, the disk's reprise finishes the operation with no job (§14.2), which `catalog.FinishCreate` records as an `ok` copy by Atoll, never verified, when the file still has a row, and not at all when it has none.

Through a deletion (`FlawJobDroppedGone.cfg`, `replica-of-deleted-file.json`):

1. A file of version 1 on disk 1; its replica's operation reaches `target-synced` on disk 2, which is then lost (`EIO`): the operation stays planned, the job running.
2. The user deletes the file's copy on disk 1.
3. A pass walks disk 1: gone. Disk 2 is missing, and its pending copy is no copy: the decision is Gone, and the file goes with its running job.
4. Disk 2 comes back. Its reprise finds the copy at its name and finishes the operation: no job, no file, nothing recorded.
5. Atoll's copy sits in the library unknown to the catalog (`Inv11_OwnOpsNotUsers` broken): the next walk records it as a user's new file, and the file the user deleted is back, with no trash.

Through the plan (`FlawJobDroppedPlan.cfg`, `replica-of-old-version.json`): the same lost disk; the plan cancels its pending copy and the running job; the user writes version 2 into the copy on disk 1, and the modification, which waits for a running job (§15.4), finds none and is decided; back, disk 2's reprise records its copy of version 1 as `ok`, by Atoll, at the file's level (`ReplicaInSync` broken): one real copy of version 2, where the catalog counts two, until a scrub reads it.

In the code: `internal/solver/plan.go` (`counts`: a pending copy on a disk `missing` is canceled), `catalog.cancelPending` (deletes the job whatever its state), the foreign key of `jobs` (`ON DELETE CASCADE`), and `internal/ops/recover.go` (`recoverCreate` with no job).

The remedy (`running-job`, approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-19): what §14.1 says, everywhere: a pending copy whose job is running is kept, and counts, as a copy on a disk not yet past `missing_grace` does; and a file with a job running waits for its decisions, as its modifications already do (§15.4), until the reprise concludes the operation. The other, a reprise that finds no job sending the copy to the trash, was not chosen. As it landed: the caller tells the plan which pending copies have their job running; such a copy counts as an `ok` copy on its disk would, so no longer past `missing_grace`, nor on a disk `draining`, `failed` or `retired`, holds no room, and is never canceled, by the level, the room, the file leaving or its disk; every decision of its file waits, Rename included, but not a file that only follows a folder renamed; and nothing else takes the file, the job or its pending copy away: a file whose last copy goes otherwise keeps that pending copy and its job (`catalog.dropFileIfEmpty`), as the model's `Emptied` does, and a modification keeps it too.

What it was (1,832 states, `ReplicaInSync`): §17.2 said "`kind = link` finishes when the link is there with its inode, its file's names taking the change time they find now, as what it had before is gone with the crash" (`ops.recoverMakeLink`; `recoverDropLink` likewise for a link's removal).

1. A file replicated from disk 1 to disk 2, the link guard on; a pass journals the link of the copy on disk 2, with up to a thousand others (`catalog.BeginLinks`).
2. The link is made, which moves the copy's change time, and Atoll stops before the batch's commit (kill -9, a power cut once the folder is synced, or the disk lost at its sync).
3. Before the reprise, or between the journal and the link as TLC's trace has it, a program rewrites that copy keeping its size and its dates, as a tagger may: only its change time moves.
4. The reprise finds the link with its inode: done, and the copy's row takes the change time it finds, the write's with the link's: every look finds the copy as its row says, the write never reaches the other copy, and a scrub reads it as corruption and repairs it away with the copy on disk 1 (invariant 9, G4), as flaw 1 did of a copy's rename.

In a normal run the commit takes the change time only when the row recorded the one just before the link, so a write since reads as an edit. `TestFlawAWriteKeepingTheDatesIsHiddenByTheRepriseOfALink` (then `internal/jobs/flaws_test.go`, Atoll stopped at the crash point `linked`, the write while it is down) failed on the code; the same write after a link that committed reaches the other copy (a control, run once and not kept).

Remedies: (a) `link-keep`: the reprise leaves the rows' change time as they recorded it, so the next look finds it moved and reads the copy, a change of metadata alone when the content is the same, a read for each copy a stopped batch left so; such a copy changed together with another changed copy of its file before that read is a conflict of §15.4 rather than a modification (`GuardRewrite.cfg` passes with it, 451,650 states, and every instance of the guard takes it); (b) `link-doubt`: the reprise takes the change time it finds and records the copy unverified, as a copy's reprise with no record does (§14.2), so that a scrub reads a mismatch as a conflict, never a corruption (435,972 states).

## Modelling mistakes, found by TLC and fixed

TLC's first traces also showed where the model was wrong, not `specs.md`:

- a user deleting a fresh copy after Atoll synced it read as a commit before the syncs: the check now asks that a name still holding what Atoll put there be durable;
- a part in `tmp/` counted as a copy of the user's version, so a file deleted while its replica was being made read as lost by Atoll: only the library and the trash count (§17, Limits);
- Atoll kept writing to a disk lost and mounted again through the handles of the old mount, which answer `EIO` in reality (§10): a session now holds the mount it confirmed;
- a check of Atoll's own moves outlived a remount that put the copy back, then deleted by the user: it now looks where the file is;
- the liveness instance ran out of operation ids, after which nothing can be journaled: it has more.

On 2026-11-22, with the SSD cache's transfer and the link guard:

- what a user's action destroys was counted on durable names alone, so that a version the transfer had already moved to its trash, the source's name not yet synced, read as Atoll's to keep when the user then edited its last other copy: a version a user's action leaves only in an entry the transfer purges at once, or that the kernel no longer shows at another name, is now the user's to lose (`UserLost`), with the cache alone;
- a source copy a replica was verified against kept its doubt (`CreateFinish`): `DoubtUnverified` found it once a reprise of a link recorded copies in doubt (the remedy `link-doubt`, which the maintainer did not choose); being read against the file's hash takes the doubt off, which no earlier instance reached;
- the walk waited at the link guard's planned operations, which `jobs.plannedOn` leaves out, as they leave the root as it is;
- the liveness instances of the cache and of the guard ran out of operation ids, in one case on the churn of observation 2, below.

On 2026-11-23, with the cache and the guard together (`CacheGuard`, 2,258,996 states): `Inv2_LastUserCopyKept` asked that a file keep a user copy after a transfer even when it had none before it, as a stop right after a hand-over, then a user's deletion of the copy the hand-over had made the user's, Kept (the copy on the cache read as changed, a link of the guard having been undone there), leaves it; invariant 2 asks only that no user copy be taken, so the check now asks that the copy moved be no user's, and that a file that had a user copy keep one.

Two observations, which break no invariant, for the maintainer: (1) a pending copy whose part a stopped copy left is planned as if none of it were written, its part's bytes counted as used and its whole size as the room it needs: on a disk its parts nearly fill, the plan cancels it, its part goes at the next tidy, and it is copied again from nothing (`Copies.tla`, met while its tidy was not yet synced); (2) in a file in `conflict` whose copy on a disk was replaced, the link guard journals at each pass an operation `link` for that copy, which `ops.MakeLink` undoes, the copy's row holding the inode of the file replaced and the look calling it there (`catalog.LinkCopies`, `gone_ns` NULL): one undone operation a pass for each such copy until the conflict is resolved.

## How sensitive it is

Each of these known-bad designs, put into the model one at a time during its writing, was caught by the invariant it breaks: the walk's confirmation without the mount id (`Inv4_UnknownNeverAbsent`), the commit of a copy right after its rename (`Inv7_PipelineOrder`), a move to the trash without the look right before it (`Inv10_DeletionOnlyUnchangedToTrash`), the source's folder synced before the trash entry's (`NoUserDataLost`), the plan without the physical disks (`Inv13_OnePhysicalDisk`), a rename that replaces what took the target (`NoUserDataLost`), and a reprise of `trash` that finishes whatever both names hold (`Inv11_OwnOpsNotUsers`). ### Flaw 6: the reprise of a folder made took a user's folder for Atoll's own

Fixed on 2026-11-22, with the remedy the maintainer chose, (a) below, within format version 1 as `ops.file_key` holds the folder's inode: `specs.md` §9, §17.2, §17.4, `ops.Disk.MakeRootFolder`, which journals the inode right after the `mkdir` and before anything is given to the folder, and `ops.recoverMkdir`, which finishes only that folder, with the crash points `mkdir-journaled` and `mkdir-unkeyed`; `TestAFolderAUserMadeIsNotTakenByTheReprise` (`internal/jobs/mkdirkey_test.go`), which failed before, `TestAFolderMadeBeforeItsInodeIsJournaledIsLeftAsMade` for what the remedy leaves, `../scenarios/replicate-crash-folder-a-user-made.json`, and `FlawRenameFolderTaken.cfg` expecting `pass`. What it was:

1. A file at A/x on disks 1 and 2. A user renames it on disk 2 into B, which they make for it.
2. A pass takes the rename to disk 1, which lacks B: the operation `mkdir` of B journaled, with the mode of disk 2's B.
3. Atoll stops before the folder is made (kill -9, a reboot; or the disk fails with `EIO` at the `mkdir`, as in the test).
4. Meanwhile a user makes B on disk 1 by hand, with another mode: their own folder, private (0700) in the test.
5. The next reprise finds a folder at B: it finishes the operation, gives the user's folder the model's mode and owner, as it holds nothing yet (0755 in the test: a private folder opened), and records it `created_by = atoll`, which §17.5 then removes once Atoll leaves it empty.

The window is Atoll's whole time down between the journal and the `mkdir`, which a disk that fails there makes as long as it is away. Remedies: (a) the operation records the folder's inode right after the `mkdir`, in a commit of its own before it gives the mode, as a copy's part records its key (§14.2), and its reprise finishes only a folder of that inode, leaving any other as a user's, the operation `undone` (`mkdir-key` in `Fixes`); a folder Atoll made and stopped before recording it is then left as it was made, owned by Atoll's account and not tidied, the cost of keeping a user's; (b) the reprise takes the mode and owner only of a folder owned by the account Atoll runs as, which is what its own `mkdir` gives it, and records any other as a user's, which needs no new record.

### Flaw 7: a file saved at a copy's old name followed Atoll's rename to the new one

Fixed on 2026-11-22, with the remedy the maintainer chose, (a) below: `specs.md` §15.12, step 4, `ops.Disk.putBack`, called by `RenameFile` when the look after the move finds what arrived is not the copy, with the crash point `put-back`; `TestAFileSavedAtTheOldNameGoesBackAfterTheRename` (`internal/jobs/renameback_test.go`), which failed before, `TestAPassKilledOnceAFileIsPutBack`, and `FlawRenameFileTaken.cfg` expecting `pass`. What it leaves: a file a user saves at the new name in the width between the move and that look is put back at the old name too, as nothing tells the two apart. What it was:

1. A file at A/x on disks 1 and 2. A user renames it to B/x on disk 2; a pass takes the rename to disk 1: the operation `rename` journaled, and the look right before finds the copy at A/x and nothing at B/x.
2. In the moment between that look and `renameat2`, a program saves a new file at A/x on disk 1, an atomic save, as one that imports a new release at the old name does.
3. `rename(2)` moves what A/x holds then: the new file goes to B/x.
4. The look after finds at B/x a file that is not the copy: the table says target `other`, source `absent`, undone, and what is at the target stays. The user's file is at a name Atoll gave it, beside where the file of B/x is on the other disk, and the next walks take it for a copy of that file, of other content.

The window is the width of a `rename(2)` after a `stat`, and needs a program writing directly to the disk while a pass renames; on a disk without `RENAME_NOREPLACE` the look at the source after the `link` catches it, and the link goes. Remedies: (a) at once after the move, a file at the target that the move itself took from the old name, when the old name is free again, goes back there without replacing anything, synced, as §17.2 has a move to the trash put back what took its source's name (`rename-back` in `Fixes`); a reprise cannot tell such a file from one a user put at the target, and leaves it, as now; (b) nothing, as the window is that narrow, and the next walks see a conflict, every version kept.

### Modelling mistakes, found by TLC and fixed

- A rename's commit first had its names durable as the move left them, whatever a user did after the syncs: a rename back by the user right after them read as a commit before the syncs. As in `Replication.tla`, a name must be durable only while it still holds what Atoll put there (`NamesDurable`).
- On a disk without `RENAME_NOREPLACE`, a source that no longer held the copy at the look after the `link` left the link at the target when the source held another file: the code removes the link it made (`moveNoReplace`), and so does the model now, by what it linked.
- `RenamesAreUsers` first took a copy that caught up with its file's own path, as after that link, for a rename: §15.12 says it is none, a file's own path being free to it.

## Copies at once: Copies.tla

A model of its own, `Copies.tla` with `MCCopies.tla` (2026-11-22), as `Replication.tla` runs one copy at a time, as the conformance build does (§29.4), and copies at once need the steps of several copies interleaved, and the room of a disk counted: each of its instances names it, `\* module: MCCopies.tla`. **One file to several disks, with refreshes** (2026-11-23, `MaxEdit`): a user writes into a copy in place, a new content of the same size, at any moment. After the reprise, a pass looks at each disk of its session and decides the modification (§15.4): one content among the changed copies, read as it has the file's size, makes them current and verified, every other copy stale and unverified, with a trash job from the first changed copy by position, and drops every queued job of the file with its pending copy; several contents, or a stale copy changed, a conflict, never queued; a file with a job running waits. ### Flaw 12: the room past min_free of a disk is shared by the daemon's copies and the view's parts

Fixed on 2026-11-22, with both remedies below, as the maintainer chose, model-checked together: `specs.md` §14, §14.1, §14.2 and §21.2; (a) `view-room` in `writer.openPart`, the room it measures on a disk less what the catalog's pending copies there still have to write, each its size less what its part holds (`catalog.PendingOn`, `ops.Disk.PartSize`, `writer.pendingLeft`); (b) `copy-room` in the copy (`replicator.spaceOf`, `room.keeps`), each write that grows its part checking `min_free` first, by the disk's free space then, or, when less, by what it was at the copy's first look less what the copy wrote since, and ending `no-room` past it, a free space that cannot be read letting the write through: in qemu, `test/loopdev`'s `TestACopyStopsAtMinFreeOnEachDriver` found that btrfs counts a write in its free space only once it is flushed, so that the disk's word alone let a copy write on past `min_free` there (`TestACopyCountsWhatItWroteWhereItsDiskDoesNotYet`). Tests, each seen failing before: `TestTheViewsPartsLeaveTheRoomAPendingCopyHolds` (`internal/view/writer/parallelroom_test.go`) and `TestACopyStopsBeforeItTakesItsDiskPastMinFree` (`internal/jobs/copyroom_test.go`), with `TestTheViewsPartsTakeTheRoomAPendingCopyNoLongerNeeds`, `TestACopyStoppedAsItDropsForMinFree`, kill -9 before and after its part goes, and `TestACopyGoesOnWhenItsDiskSpaceCannotBeRead`; `FlawParallelRoom.cfg` expects `pass`, 235 states, as `ParallelView`, 228,140, the same as with `view-room` alone: with it, no part takes the room a copy holds, and `copy-room` keeps `min_free` against any other writer Atoll has, which these pools do not hold. 1. A pass's plan queues two copies to the SSD disk 3, their pending copies holding all its room past `min_free`.
2. The copies run at once; meanwhile a new file written through Atoll's own view gets a part on disk 3, whose room is the disk's free space less `min_free`.

### How sensitive it is

Each put in on its own: the plan placing a copy with no room past `min_free` (`MinFreeKept`, in `ParallelTight`, 358 states); the plan forgetting the room the copies it placed before hold (`MinFreeKept`, `ParallelTight`, 962); a copy going on through the handle of a mount it lost, as one beside it found the disk failing (`Inv7_PipelineOrder`, `Parallel`, 3,330); and the tidy of `tmp/` not synced (`MinFreeKept`, `Parallel`, met while the model was written: parts the tidy removed came back after a power cut beside a copy the plan made in their room). Not caught: each disk's limit of copies at once taken away (`Parallel`, 1,957,109 states, the same), as in these pools a disk with a spindle has one copy reading it and the SSD's limit is the number of copies there, a matter of speed, not safety; and the per-path lock, which `OneCopyOfAFile` checks by its definition alone, as a file here has one job at a time.

Flaw 12's remedies, 2026-11-22: both taken out, `MinFreeKept` in `FlawParallelRoom` (129 states) and in `ParallelView` (2,802); `view-room` alone taken out, nothing (688 and 497,078 states), `copy-room` keeping `min_free` by itself; `copy-room` alone taken out, nothing (235 and 228,140), as in these pools nothing but the view's parts and a user's own file, which `MinFreeKept` excuses, takes the room the plan counted on.

### What it leaves out

What a walk finds and the decisions beyond a copy changed in place (no deletion, replacement or rename), the debounce and settle (a source found changed waits for the next pass), the purge of the trash, folders, and the lock of a disk's names (each step that makes or removes a name is one step here, as the lock makes it in the code), more than one part a file, of the view's parts the room alone (their journal and adoption are `Replication.tla`'s), a chunk at a time (a copy writes its bytes in one step) and a filesystem that counts a write in its free space only once it is flushed, the window of 4,096 jobs, and time.

## Folders renamed across disks: Folders.tla

A model of its own, `Folders.tla` with `MCFolders.tla` (2026-11-23), for what `Rename.tla` leaves out: a folder renamed on one disk whose copies are on others, the job that carries it to each, a chain of renames on a disk, and a cycle that only an exchange of two names gets round. It keeps folder names at the root (D, E, F) and file names in a folder (x, y), each instance naming how many; on each of two disks the folders at the root and the files in each folder, by object, as the kernel shows them and as a power cut leaves them, each folder's names made durable by its own sync, an exchange's two folders by one; the catalog's files and copies, each where it sits and with the kind of clock its last look started (`gone_kind`), the entries of the folders, the folder jobs of each disk (`kind = rename`, `file_id` NULL) and the journal (a rename of a file or of a folder, and `mkdir`, here in one step: `Rename.tla` has its steps). A user, directly on a disk: renames a folder, swaps two folders' names, moves a file (its folder made), swaps two files' names, makes, replaces or deletes a file, makes or removes a folder. Atoll, one pass after another: the mount check and the reprise (§17.2: a file by the identity of §15.1, a folder by its inode, and a folder's commit as `FinishFolderRename(op, 0)` gives it in a reprise); the walk of each disk, its confirmation, its verdicts and the folders found renamed (`watch.moveFolders`: a folder row whose folder is not at its name, found at a name with no row, its old name holding no file at home; its row stays, and its new name and what is below it get none until decided), a place holding another row's copy read as gone, as the code reads it; the decisions about the files, Rename by its greatest fixed point, a job above a copy whose `old_lpath` a rename back clears dropped (`catalog.dropJobsAbove`); then the folders found renamed, in the order of their jobs, each waiting while its disk still holds copies below its old name, but for those no pass can take from there, one no copy came with only its entries and the jobs it moves on (`catalog.moveFolderJobs`), and otherwise the files below it that only other disks hold following it, its entries, the jobs moved on and a job for each other disk with a copy below it (`jobs.decideFolder`); then on each disk by position its folder jobs, in the order of `jobs.folderOrder`, the first of a cycle dropped, each run when ready (`folderReady`, `landsBadly`) as one operation, journaled, the look right before by the folder's inode, `RENAME_NOREPLACE`, the look after, the sync and the commit, anything else resolved at once by the table (`trash.Session.AfterFailedMove`), the job dropped when not ready, its copies then following one by one; then the renames by old path, round after round until one moves nothing, each as `Rename.tla`'s with the put-back of flaw 7; and once a round moves nothing, the copies left path-taken whose targets lead round a cycle, the first by place and the copy at its target exchanged (`jobs.exchange`, `ops.Disk.ExchangeFiles`): both journaled, the look right before at both names, the exchange of what both hold then, the look after at both, the sync of both folders and the two commits.

### Flaw 13: a file saved at one of two names right before an exchange follows it to the other

1. Files a at D/x and b at D/y on disks 1 and 2. A user swaps their names on disk 1; a pass renames both, and on disk 2 each copy is path-taken at the other's name: the exchange, both journaled, the look right before finds each copy at its name.
2. In the moment between that look and `renameat2`, a program saves a file at D/y on disk 2 (an atomic save; TLC's trace has the user swap the two names there instead, which does the same).
3. The exchange swaps what the names hold: the saved file goes to D/x, a's copy to D/y.
4. The look after finds D/y holding a's copy and D/x not b's: the table of §17.2 finishes a's rename and undoes b's, and what is at b's target, the file saved, stays there, at D/x, a name Atoll gave it (invariant 15); in TLC's trace a user's own swap there is undone by Atoll. The remedies proposed then, each checked against every invariant: (a) `exchange-back`: when the look after finds that a name does not hold the copy it should, and both names still hold what the exchange gave them, exchange them back at once, synced, and undo both operations, as flaw 7's remedy puts back what a rename took (`Exchange`, `SwapDirs`, `ExchangeLive` passed with it, and so did `FlawExchangeSaved`, 2,076 states); (b) nothing: the window is that narrow, and every version is kept.

### Modelling mistakes, found by TLC and fixed

- A folder job ran above a copy whose rename back the same pass decided, and carried it away again, which the next walk read as a user's rename (`RenamesAreUsers`, `FolderJob`, 1,269,744 states): the code drops such a job in the decision's own transaction (`catalog.dropJobsAbove`, §15.12 "So does a job above a copy whose `old_lpath` a decision clears"), and so does the model now.
- A copy whose place holds another row's copy, as a half-resolved exchange leaves two rows at one place, read as `replaced` by that copy: the code reads it as gone, a name the file had left, and the decision is Kept (observation 3, below), and so does the model now.
- `RenamesAreUsers` read as Atoll's a file a user saved into a folder on a disk whose job then carried it to the folder's new name (1,434,452 states), which §15.12 reads "as a user's rename would be": it now takes the latest name a user gave the file's folder as a user's move, and so still finds flaw 16, whose user took that name back.
- An invariant of two rows at one place, once nothing is planned, which the first run of `Exchange` broke (231,441 states) on a trace that the next walk resolves as a user's own swap would: dropped, observation 3 below.

### Observation 3: an exchange resolved half way leaves two rows at one place

Not a flaw, for the maintainer: an exchange stopped once made and before its two commits (Atoll killed, or a disk lost), then resolved one operation at a time by the table of §17.2, where a user has deleted, saved or swapped one of the two names meanwhile, finishes one rename and undoes the other, whose row stays at the place the first one's copy now holds; or with only the first operation journaled and a user making the same swap by hand, the first finishes. §17.2 says "No row is ever said to be at a name it is not at". The next walk reads that row's copy gone with its `old_lpath`, a name the file had left: Kept, the file copied back to that disk, or with a file saved at its name a `conflict`, every version kept, as a user's own swap of the two names would read. A temporary test of the code (not kept) showed it: two rows of a disk at one place after the reprise, then the deletion Kept and the save a conflict. 4. The view presents nothing at D, renamed from; the catalog presents the folder D of disk 1. The operation is never taken in (`EventuallySettled`), and the empty D stays on disk 1, where the view never shows it.

The remedies proposed, each checked against every invariant: (a) `view-moot`, flaw 20's, whose part for a folder whose old name holds a folder again on a disk with no job to move it takes the operation in, the catalog then presenting the empty D, which a user can remove (`FlawViewFolderTaken` passed with it, 397 states); (b) the view refusing the rename, `EEXIST`, when a disk that holds the folder has anything at the new name, which it would not present: tried on an earlier version of the model, it was not enough alone (`FlawViewFolderTaken` still broke `EventuallySettled`), as a user may make E right after that look; with (a), not needed.

### Flaw 22: a folder's job decided before a rename back through the view carries the folder from the name the view gave it

Open. §15.12, Folders: a folder renamed on one disk gives each other disk that holds it a job, which "runs when D is a folder on that disk, the one its last walk found there by its inode"; §21.2: "A pass waits at the paths of the view's operations under way". A folder's operation is journaled on its first holder alone, so a walk of another holder does not wait for it (`jobs.viewPlannedOn`), and `jobs.folderReady` asks nothing of the view.

1. D, with D/x, on disks 1 and 2. The view renames D to E on both: done.
2. The view renames E back to D: journaled on disk 1, its first holder, and made there; atoll mount is stopped short before disk 2, and disk 1 is lost. The rename back stays planned, on disk 1 alone.
3. The passes walk disk 2, which nothing of the view holds, and find the first rename there, D to E, twice: decided, a job D to E queued for disk 1, away, from its entry of D.
4. Disk 1 comes back; the next pass runs the job before anything resolves the rename back: disk 1's D, the folder its entry records, goes to E, the name the view had taken it from (`ViewNamesKept`). The view's check then finds no folder at D on disk 1 and calls the rename back undone; the folder is at E on both disks, and the user's rename back is lost, as after a stop it may be, but by Atoll's own job, not by the stop.

TLC's trace is the same with the view slow rather than stopped. The test, `TestFlawAFolderJobCarriesAFolderFromTheNameTheViewGaveItBack`, stops the rename back between the disks (a panic at t2's first call, standing for kill -9), makes t1 missing for three passes, then brings it back: its folder is at `Shows/E`. The remedy proposed, checked against every invariant: (a) `job-view-held`: a folder's job decided before an operation of the view at, below or above its names, planned when the pass decided it or journaled since, done or not and taken in or not, goes when it would run, read in the transaction that would journal its rename, for a later pass to decide anew from what the disks hold; and a copy's rename by a pass waits for one planned when the pass began or journaled since, one done before, or that is the same rename, being what the job carries out (`FlawViewFolderJobBack` passed with it, 22,928 states).

### Observation 4: what the view presents may not open until the next pass

Not a flaw, for the maintainer: `ViewReadable`, that a file the view presents can be opened at its path on the disk it presents it from, or, of a file it presents from the catalog, on a disk there holding a copy at its path, once nothing of the view is planned and no user acted directly, fails in two ways the next pass ends: a folder renamed through the view while a holder had no handle presents its files at the new name from the catalog's rows below the old one, which that disk holds at the old name until the pass takes the rename there (§21.2, "a pass takes it to a disk it missed"); and an operation done whose take-in a later planned one holds back. Each is the window of one pass. No instance checks `ViewReadable`.

### Modelling mistakes, found by TLC and fixed

- A folder's rename counted done only when its first holder held it at the new name: §21.2 says "done once it reached one", and the journal's truth is now any holder's (`ViewJournalTrue`).
- A replacement whose source is gone once its copy replaced is in the trash read as a journal that lies: the code leaves that to finish (`resolveReplace`, "what was replaced is in the trash, which is all there is to finish"), and so does the model now.
- `ViewJournalTrue` flagged a verdict of `undone` on a move a user made directly, or after a user changed a name the view's verdict reads by names: it now asks the truth of `done` of what the view did not make, and of `undone` only of what it made while no user acted directly.
- What the view presents below a folder renamed took the catalog's rows at the old name alone: `view.Tree` reads them below the new name too, once a pass recorded the rename, and so does the model.
- The holders of a folder were those of its last name only: `writer.folderHolders` reads every path it resolves to.
- The take-in compared a file presented from another copy than the one the view wrote: §21.2 takes it in once the catalog holds that copy in sync (`view.inSyncOn`).
- A folder's job was modelled as copies following one by one: the code runs it as one operation when ready, its copies following one by one only when it goes (`jobs.runFolderJob`), and the walks move a job on when its target is renamed (`catalog.moveFolderJobs`).
- Every change a walk found was decided at once, by the walk of that disk alone: the code decides a copy's change only once its clock is a debounce old, after every walk of the pass, waiting while another copy of the file is at a name its disk's walk waited at or shows a change not yet due (`pass.isDue`, `solver.Decide`, `folderWaits`), and a folder found renamed keeps its entry until then. Without that, TLC found a job decided from a walk older than the view's rename (`ViewNamesKept`), which the code's test showed no pass can make (2026-11-23): the debounce, the waits and the walk's waits recorded for the pass (`pc.w`) are in the model now.
- A folder's job ran on whatever folder was at its old name: since flaw 14's remedy it runs only on the one its disk's entry records there (`jobs.folderReady`, `catalog.EntryKeyAt`), and so does the model.

### How sensitive it is

Each part of a remedy taken out on its own, in a copy of the module not kept, once the model was as above, the other remedies kept: of `view-moot`, the part for a folder's rename none of whose holders holds what it named at the new name any more (`EventuallySettled` in `ViewFoldersUserLive`, 5,292 states: a folder's rename through the view stopped after its first holder, then moved back directly there, never taken in, flaw 20 for a folder), and the part for a folder whose old name holds a folder again (`EventuallySettled` in `ViewFoldersUserLive`, 5,132, and in flaw 21's pool, 373); of `job-view-held`, the folder's job going (`ViewNamesKept` in `ViewFolders`, 24,214, `ViewFoldersUser`, 50,488, and flaw 22's pool, 6,860), and the copy's rename waiting (`ViewJournalTrue` in `ViewFolders`, 62,650: a pass's rename of a copy behind, decided from the first rename, carries it from the folder the rename back gave its name on that disk, making the first's folder again there, and the reprise of the rename back, left planned, then calls it undone); counting the operations a pass took in already: nothing broke without it, in any instance above. Each remedy whole, taken out, gives its flaw back in its pool (the `Flaw…` instances, `Fixes` empty).

### What it leaves out

The daemon's own journal and its crashes (each of its operations one step here; `Folders.tla` and `Replication.tla` have their steps); the guards and the settle; what a user makes new being recorded as a file of its own; the power cut (every name durable as it is made); refused and colliding names; folders below folders; the view's other operations (`Replication.tla`'s and `ViewMove.tla`'s); more than two disks; a folder's mode.

## Keeping the model and specs.md in step

`fingerprints.json` lists each section of `specs.md` the model formalizes, with the SHA-256 of its text: from its heading to the next heading of the same level or a higher one, so a section holds its subsections; CRLF read as LF, each line without its trailing spaces and tabs, headings inside fenced code blocks not counting, blank lines at the end dropped, each line ended by LF (its `normalisation` says so in full). `go test ./conformance/models` fails when a section no longer has that text, and says to reread against it the modules that cite it, among the `models` listed there, update them or confirm them unchanged, and put the new fingerprint in the same commit (`CLAUDE.md`, "Rust-rewrite readiness"). It also fails when no module cites a section it pins. Each section pinned says so in a sentence that points here.
